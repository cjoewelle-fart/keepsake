# Naming round 13: app-store terms (Oct 28, 2026)

The owner asked us to "research the key terms used in app galleries (like 'sort', 'organize') so we can leverage terms already commonly used, then think of brand names around them." The Product owner looked at names, subtitles and listings on the App Store, Mac App Store, Google Play and Microsoft Store. The PO also looked at how people describe the task ("sort photos by date", "photorec sort"), then made up 41 names and checked each one with web searches (apps, software brands, US marks in classes 9/42). None of them were used or dropped in rounds 1–11. These are web searches, not legal clearance, and we had no tool that shows search volume, so "crowded" below means how many listings turned up. The attorney search still comes before the beta (phase 4).

## Findings first

1. **A new close competitor: Sortrix** (Microsoft Store, "Sortrix: photo manager"). It sorts photos by year from the date they were taken, finds duplicate and similar photos "so you can remove them", and can group faces. It says it is "fully offline & private". It isn't built for PhotoRec output, and removing duplicates goes against **Never destructive**. Add it to `competitors.md`. Whether it changes gate 1 goes to the owner with D22.
2. **"Sort" alone means puzzle games.** Water Sort, Ball Sort and "Calm Sorting" games fill every "sort" search. "Sort" works in a name only if the descriptor says "photo".
3. **"Keep" is becoming a cleaner word.** Examples: "Photo: Keep or Delete", KeepFirst: Photo Cleaner, Keep&Go: AI Photo Cleaner, and Keepers ("Keep only your best photos"). "Keep" now often means the keep-or-delete swipe. This affects Keeplight, Keepbubble and Keepsake (see What to test).
4. **Words in the app name don't have to carry the search terms.** Apple indexes the name, the subtitle (30 characters) and a hidden 100-character keyword field. Google Play indexes the title and descriptions. Apple's own advice is "avoid generic terms". Under US law a merely descriptive name like "Photo Organizer" can't be registered without proof of acquired distinctiveness. Most top apps use a distinctive name plus a search-term subtitle ("Cleanup: Phone Storage Cleaner").

**Philippines:** spelling follows American English, so use "organize" and "color". The audience for cleaner apps there is large (about 17.6M users, mostly 18–24, mostly on Vivo, Oppo and Realme phones), so "clean" there strongly means deleting. "Gallery" is the stock Android word for the photo app. Ask Filipino testers what they call it before using it in copy.

## Name or descriptor

- **In the name:** one honest action or result word (sort, year, line), made distinctive.
- **In the descriptor and keywords:** photo, organizer, by date, recovered, PhotoRec, folders, offline, library.

The app is a web app, so the descriptor goes in the page title, meta description and GitHub topics at launch (phase 5). Store listings come with the desktop app after launch.

**Descriptor options (each fits Apple's 30-character subtitle):**
1. "Sort recovered photos by date" (29). Recommended.
2. "Photo organizer by date" (23)
3. "Sort PhotoRec files by date" (27), for the family IT person and repair shops

Google Play short description (76 of 80 characters): "Sorts PhotoRec photos into dated folders. Nothing uploaded, nothing deleted."

## Dropped (35)

- **Taken in our field:**
  - PhotoSorter: a Mac app that sorts photos into date folders.
  - Photosort, Picsort, Picasort: existing photo-sorting and gallery-cleaner apps.
  - Photorder: Photo Order, a photo-printing app.
  - Keepalbum: Keep&Go, an album organizer and cleaner.
  - Albumkeeper: Keeper: Photo Assistant, plus album-locker apps.
  - Photoshelf: PhotoShelter.
  - Yeargrid: Year Grid and "Your Year: 365 Photo Grid".
  - Photoyears: "Photo Year – Date the Pic".
  - Keepyear: Keepy, an app for archiving children's keepsakes.
- **Taken elsewhere:**
  - Sortwise: a SortWise Shopify app.
  - Sortly: an inventory app with SORTLY marks.
  - Sortify: an inventory app, and Sortify.tm, a trademark firm.
  - Daysort: the Sorted and Sortd planner apps.
  - Dayline: several planner apps.
  - Datekeeper: Date Keeper and DayKeeper apps.
  - Keepdated: the KeepDates app.
  - Orderly: order-tracking apps, and it's also a hospital job.
  - Shelfwise: a company called ShelfWise.
  - Sortshelf: an itch.io game, plus a Shelf Sort app.
  - Sortmark: a browser extension that sorts bookmarks.
- **Wrong meaning:**
  - Datewise: a dating journal.
  - Calmsort: "Calm Sorting" puzzle games.
  - Everyear: heard as "every ear", and searches return ear piercing.
  - Photodrawer: apps for drawing on photos.
  - Albumline, Albumwise: music-album apps.
- **Generic or weak:**
  - Datesort, Yearsort, Photo Organizer, In Order: descriptive, so they can't be protected. "In order" is also a computer-science term.
  - Photo Librarian: five syllables, and generic.
  - Yearwise: year-progress apps, and "wise" adds nothing.
  - Sortkeeper: clunky, with the Sorticker app as a neighbour.

## The team's top 2

1. **Sortwell.** It puts the app's real job ("sort") in the name, says it gently, and its search is clean. With "Sort recovered photos by date" beside it, the name and subtitle cover the top search terms without a destructive or upload meaning (**Calm and plain**, **Never destructive**).
2. **Yearline.** It names the result (years in order) in the plainest words of any round, which suits Filipino and non-technical users. It's the plain-word cousin of Ringwood.

## How they compare with the current shortlist

- **Sortwell vs Photocomb:** both say what the app does, but Sortwell uses the word people actually search for and has no silent b. If Photocomb fails hear-it-type-it, Sortwell is its replacement. **Add Sortwell to the D21 test.**
- **Yearline vs Ringwood:** the same idea. Ringwood carries a story and is easier to protect. Yearline is plainer and more generic. Ringwood stays ahead, and Yearline is its backup. Don't test both in one session.
- **Photofern:** it is about a feeling and carries no search terms, so it relies fully on the descriptor.
- **Keeplight and Keepbubble:** finding 3 is a new risk for both. Keepbubble stays the reserve.

## What to test (add to D21, T44 rules)

- **"What do you think this app does?"** A name fails if 2 or more of 5 testers say it recovers, deletes, cleans or uploads photos. For Sortwell, also ask "Is this a game?" For every Keep- name, also ask "Would this app delete photos you don't keep?"
- **Hear it, type it.** Pass: 6 of 8 type it exactly, with at least 3 of 4 Filipino testers.
- **Keepsake stays the control.**

The name is the owner's call (D21), and it must be settled before the beta (phase 4).
