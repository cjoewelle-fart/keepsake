# Naming round 10: things that regrow or come back (Oct 27, 2026)

The owner asked for names from "animals or other living things that somehow regenerate or are reborn", combined with "photo" or a product word. The Product owner matched creatures and plants to the app's features, made up 41 names, and checked each one with web searches (apps, software brands, US marks in classes 9/42, photo, backup and recovery products). The list leaves out every name used or dropped in rounds 1–9. These are web searches, not legal clearance. The attorney search still comes before the beta (phase 4).

## Finding first: "reborn" over-promises

Keepsake doesn't recover or repair anything. PhotoRec does the recovery. Keepsake finds the real photos in the pile, sets junk aside without deleting it, and puts the photos in date order. Names like "bring back to life", "regrow" or "restore" promise what voice.md bans ("restore, rescue (as a promise)"). They also set up the person whose photo is damaged to feel let down.

**The honest angle is the resurrection fern.** It never actually dies. In a drought it curls up brown, and after rain it unrolls green within a day. Your photos are the same: they were in the recovered pile all along, and Keepsake makes them visible again and puts them in order. Use lines like "Green again after the drought" or "They were here all along." Never use "We brought your photos back to life."

## Map: living things, matched to features and feelings

| Living thing | Trait | Feature or feeling | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Resurrection fern, moss | Dries out, greens again with water; was never dead | Photos were in the pile all along, now seen / relief | **Best, and honest** |
| Perennials, bulbs | Come back every spring | Same photos, a second season / calm | Good |
| Fiddlehead (new fern frond) | Unrolls a spiral | The timeline unrolling by date | Good, as a visual |
| Axolotl, newt, salamander, starfish, gecko | Regrow lost parts | Implies we rebuild what's missing | **Over-promises repair** |
| Phoenix | Reborn from ashes | Implies a total rescue from disaster | **Over-promises**; data-recovery brands use it |
| Butterfly, chrysalis | Transforms | Implies photos get changed | **Fights** "nothing changed" |
| Snake, crab, deer | Shed skin, shell or antlers | Throwing things off | **Fights** "Never destructive" |
| Lotus | Rises clean from mud | "Clean" is banned for set-aside files | **Fights** |
| Tardigrade | Survives anything | Toughness, not care | Weak |
| Sprout, seed | New growth | New, not your old photos | Weak |

## Dropped (36)

- **Taken in our field:**
  - Phoenix, Photophoenix, Phoenix Photos: Stellar Phoenix Photo Recovery and Phoenix Photo Editor.
  - Pixsprout: an AI image tool.
  - Sprout: Sprout Social marks, and Sprout Studio (software for photographers).
  - Tardigrade: Storj's *cloud* storage.
  - Starfish, Sea star: Starfish Storage (file-management software) and several STARFISH software marks.
  - Salamander: Altap/Open Salamander, a file manager.
- **Taken elsewhere:**
  - Axolotl, Axolotl Photos: Axolotl, a well-known open-source AI training tool, fills the searches.
  - Gecko: Mozilla's browser engine.
  - Hydra: THC Hydra, a password-cracking tool.
  - Firefly: Adobe Firefly.
  - Bamboo: Wacom Bamboo and Atlassian Bamboo.
  - Chrysalis: a CHRYSALIS application covering software and SaaS. The word is also easy to misspell.
  - Seedling: a SEEDLING mark and several seedling apps.
  - Regrow: Regrow Ag. The word also promises regrowth.
  - Lotus: a Lotus AI photo animator, plus the "clean" problem.
  - Molt, Moult: shedding, and the Moultrie camera app.
- **Wrong meaning:**
  - Resurrect, Resurrection Fern: promises revival, and the word carries heavy religious weight in the Catholic Philippines.
  - Jericho, Rose of Jericho: the walls that fell (destruction), and a luck charm.
  - Butterfly: change.
  - Shed: discarding.
  - Antler: shed every year, and hunting.
  - Coral: bleaching means dying.
  - Hydra: also a many-headed monster.
  - Rebloom: promises the photos get remade, and the Reblum app is a neighbour.
- **Weak, cute or hard to spell:**
  - Pixolotl: double spelling risk, and "pix" is casual.
  - Planarian: a flatworm few people know.
  - Newt: slimy, and UK slang for drunk.
  - Moss, Photomoss: "gathers no moss" (neglect), and dampness.
  - Perennial: often misspelled "Perenial", four syllables, and "comes back" is a promise.
  - Green Again: reads as an eco app.
  - Afterrain: weather apps.
  - Rainfern, Fernlight: weaker twins of Photofern and Keeplight. A Fern habit app is a neighbour.
  - Photosprout: "sprout" means new, not your old photos.

## The team's top 2

1. **Photofern.** It is the only regrowth image that is honest: the fern was never dead, and neither were the photos in the pile. It is as easy to say as any name we've had (no silent b), its search is clean, and the fiddlehead gives a calm logo that echoes the unrolling timeline.
2. **Keepfern.** It has the same story plus "keep", but only if it passes hear-it-type-it, because the "p-f" clash is the exact flaw the owner raised about Keepfold. If it fails, **Second Spring** takes its place.

**The axolotl** works better as an optional empty-screen mascot under any name, never in copy that suggests repair. The owner can decide that after launch (phase 5).

## How they compare with the current shortlist

- **Photofern vs Photocomb:** both have the "Photo-" shape. Photocomb says what the app does ("combs through"). Photofern says how it feels ("green again") and avoids the silent b. Photofern is the first name since round 8 that might beat Photocomb, so **add it to the D21 test**.
- **Keepfern vs Keeplight:** Keeplight is easier to say and searches just as cleanly. Keepfern doesn't beat it.
- **Keepbubble stays the reserve.** If Photocomb fails the hear-it-type-it test, Photofern is its natural replacement.

## What to test (add to D21, T44 rules)

- **"What do you think this app does?"** A name fails if 2 or more of 5 testers say it recovers, repairs, deletes or cleans photos. For fern names, also ask "Is this a gardening app?"
- **Hear it, type it.** Pass: 6 of 8 type it exactly, with at least 3 of 4 Filipino testers. This test decides Keepfern.
- **Keepsake stays the control.** Test no more than one "Photo-" name per session.

The name is the owner's call (D21), and it must be settled before the beta (phase 4).
