# Naming round 11: order and structure in nature (Oct 27, 2026)

The owner asked for "more names, with animals or plants, that relate to the main product features like organising and structure." The Product owner matched living things to three features: sorting nameless files into year → month → day, setting junk aside without deleting it, and giving people a calm structure they can browse. The PO then made up 42 names and checked each one with web searches (apps, software brands, US marks in classes 9/42, photo, backup and recovery products). The list leaves out every name used or dropped in rounds 1–10. Orchard and Pomegranate on their own were dropped in round 4, and Varve, Strata and the Honey-/hive names in rounds 3 and 8, so only new compounds of those appear here. These are web searches, not legal clearance. The attorney search still comes before the beta (phase 4).

## Finding first: tree rings are the best fit in any round

A tree adds one ring each year, and you can read its whole life in date order without anything being taken away. That matches the app's main screen (photos by year, month and day) and **Never destructive** (nothing is taken out). It needs no story about rescue. But the obvious word is taken: **TreeRing is a photo-yearbook company** (TreeRing Yearbooks, TREERING MEMORIES mark). That is our field, so we have to use the rings without that word.

## The team's top 2

1. **Ringwood.** Of any name in any round, it is the closest match to the app's structure: one ring per year, read in order, nothing taken away. It is easy to say and spell, no similar software turned up, and the log-rings logo is calm. It needs the descriptor ("Ringwood: photo sorter for recovered files"), and the test must rule out jewellery and doorbell readings.
2. **Heartwood.** It tells the same tree story with more warmth, but it says less about dates, and its searches are busier. It's Ringwood's backup, not a separate pick.

## How they compare with the current shortlist

- **Ringwood vs Photocomb:** Photocomb says the action (combing through the pile). Ringwood says the result (years in order) and has no silent b. They are equally strong. If Photocomb fails hear-it-type-it, Ringwood is the better replacement than Photofern for an "order" name.
- **Ringwood vs Photofern:** Photofern is about feeling (green again), and Ringwood is about structure. The owner asked for structure this round, so Ringwood fits better. Don't test both in one session.
- **Ringwood vs Keeplight:** both need a descriptor. Ringwood is more specific about dates, and Keeplight carries "keep" (**Never destructive**). **Add Ringwood to the D21 test.**
- **Keepbubble stays the reserve.**

## What to test (add to D21, T44 rules)

- **"What do you think this app does?"** A name fails if 2 or more of 5 testers say it recovers, repairs, deletes or cleans photos. For Ringwood, also ask "Is this a jewellery, doorbell or woodworking app?" and fail it if 2 or more of 5 say yes.
- **Hear it, type it.** Pass: 6 of 8 type it exactly, with at least 3 of 4 Filipino testers.
- **Keepsake stays the control.** Test no more than one tree name per session.

The name is the owner's call (D21), and it must be settled before the beta (phase 4).
