# Naming round 18: names with "day" (Nov 8, 2026)

The owner wrote: "how about we brainstorm for more options involving 'day' given that basically these photos are memories of user's days? thinking samples like daysort, daychive (day + archive), daykeep, daymark". The UX writer looked at her four names and made up 24 new ones in five groups. The list leaves out every name used or dropped in rounds 1–17. That includes these "day" names: Daysort, Dayline and Datekeeper/DayKeeper (round 13), Daybook (round 3), Dayreel (round 9), Dayslide (round 5), Daystamp (round 14) and Daylog (round 16).

**Important: no web searches were run this round.** The conflicts below are what the writer already knows, not a fresh screen. Every name still needs the usual web screen, the attorney's trademark search (US and Philippines, classes 9 and 42) and domain checks before it can be tested.

## Findings first

1. **The owner's idea is honest.** The app's main screen shows photos by year, month and day (round 11), and every photo is someone's day. So "day" isn't a false promise, even though the folders on disk go by year and month.
2. **But "day" is the word planners and diaries use.** Day One, Daylio, Daybook, Day Log, DayKeeper, Dayline and the Sorted planner apps all use it, and so does Dayforce (HR and payroll software). "Day" plus a verb sounds like "plan your day". "Day" plus "book", "log" or "story" sounds like "write about your day". Most names in this round lean one way or the other. The test must ask about it (see the test rules).
3. **"Days" names can promise too much.** "Alldays found 2,214 photos" sounds like every day is back. Some photos will be missing, and some land in the No date group. Avoid "all" and "every".
4. **Blends like Daychive break the hear-it, type-it test.** Blends only work when the reader can see both words. Our users usually hear the name from a friend or a repair shop, so they can't.
5. **"Day" plus "keep" or "safe" brings back old problems.** "Keep" now sounds like a cleaner word (round 13), and "safe" sounds like a locked vault (Keepsafe). The "day" doesn't fix either one.

## Tension check: is "day" fine?

**Yes, as a feeling.** The app shows every photo by year, month and day, so "day" is true. It just doesn't describe the folders on disk. But each name must not sound like a different kind of app:

| Sounds like | Names | Example apps |
|---|---|---|
| A planner or calendar | Daysort, Dayback, Daykeep (partly), Daysaved | Sorted, Sortd, DayBack, Dayline, DayKeeper |
| A diary or journal | Daystory, Thatday, Mydays, Ourdays | Day One, Daybook, Day Log, Daylio |
| A health or habit tracker | Mydays, Daysafe | My Days (period tracker), Daystamp (round 14) |
| A productivity or business tool | Dayback, Dayfiled | Dayforce (HR and payroll), Daylite (business organizer for Mac) |
| Uploading or a cloud service | Daybox, Daystory, Daysaved | Dropbox, Box, Instagram Stories |
| A promise of recovery | Dayfound, Dayback, Alldays | — |
| Writing a date on the photo | Daymark | DayMark food labels, Timemark (round 17) |
| **Fits: your days, kept in order** | **Daykept, Dayworth, Daysake, Daymark (mostly), Dayshelf** | — |

No "day" name says "photos" or "sorted" on its own. Each needs the descriptor "Sort recovered photos by date" (round 13).

## Shortlist (the team's top 5)

| Name | Idea | In a sentence | Say / spell risk | Conflicts | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Daymark** | Each day marked, and a calm marker that shows the way | "Daymark found 2,214 photos." "Daymark can't save to that folder. Your files are fine." | Low | Daymark Solutions (IT), DayMark food labels | Medium |
| **Daysake** | For old days' sake. Sister word to Keepsake | "Daysake found 2,214 photos." | Medium–high: "Day-sa-keh" | None known | Low–medium (not checked) |
| **Daykeep** | Keep each day. The owner's warmest name | "Daykeep found 2,214 photos." | Medium: "Daykip" | DayKeeper, Google Keep, "keep" cleaner meaning | Medium–high |
| **Dayworth** | A day's worth: every day is worth keeping | "Dayworth found 2,214 photos." | Medium: "Daywort" | None known | Low–medium (not checked) |
| **Daykept** | Your days, already kept | "Daykept found 2,214 photos." | Medium: "Daykep" | Near DayKeeper | Medium |

1. **Daymark** is the easiest to say and the calmest. The day theme gives it the meaning it lacked in round 17. Its risk is the date-stamp meaning, so it must pass "Would it write a date on your photos?"
2. **Daysake** joins the owner's day idea with the current name. It is a sister word to Keepsake, not a copy of it. Its risk is reading it aloud ("sa-keh"). If it passes, it is a warm, distinctive choice with no conflict the writer knows of.
3. **Daykeep** is the owner's idea at its most direct and the warmest of her four. But DayKeeper is very close, and "keep" now sounds like a cleaner word. It needs the attorney's view before any test.
4. **Dayworth** says "every day matters" without promising that every day is back. "Th" is its weak spot in Filipino English.
5. **Daykept** says "already kept", which works against the keep-or-delete meaning. Use it only if Daykeep fails, and never test the two together.

Daysort and Daychive are not on the list: Daysort was dropped in round 13, and Daychive fails hear-it, type-it both ways.

**Test rules (D21, T44), additions:**
- For any Day- name, ask "Is this a diary, planner or calendar app?" A name fails if 2 or more of 5 testers say yes.
- For Daymark, also ask "Would it write a date on your photos?" (round 17 rule).
- In hear-it, type-it, count "Dayshort", "Daykip", "Daykep", "Daywort" and "Daykive" as fails. In read-it-aloud, count "Day-sa-keh" for Daysake as a fail.
- Test no more than one Day- name per session, and never Daysake in the same session as Keepsake.

The name is the owner's call (D21), and it must be settled before the beta (phase 4).
