# Naming round 16: Photo log, Sortlist, Sortly (Nov 2, 2026)

The owner suggested three names and asked the team to assess them and come up with more names around them. The Product owner checked each one with web searches (apps, software brands, US marks in classes 9/42, photo, backup and sorting products), made up 20 related names, and screened those too. The list leaves out every name used or dropped in rounds 1–15. These are web searches, not legal clearance. The attorney search still comes before the beta (phase 4).

## Findings first

1. **Sortly is still taken, and the conflict is stronger than round 13 said.** Sortly Inc. was founded in 2013 and has about 63–70 staff. Its app has been on the App Store since about 2012. It is an inventory app built on photos: you photograph things and sort them into folders. It is also sold as a home-organizing app. Its US application SORTLY (97441185, filed 2022) covers **classes 9 and 42**, for "creating home inventory lists" and tracking home assets. That is our class, our verb, and home users like ours. Justia shows that application as new, so the attorney must confirm its current status and whether older registrations exist. A second "Sortly" also appeared in 2025, a Shopify app that sorts products. Verdict: **don't use it.**
2. **The "s/sh" sound breaks both sort puns.** Filipino English often says "sh" as "s". So "Sortlist" can be heard as "shortlist" and "Sortly" as "shortly" (meaning "soon"). The pun turns into a spelling error.
3. **"Log" names land among journal and job-site apps.** Day Log, Piclog and Photo Journal are diary apps. In construction and inspections, a "photo log" is a work document (PhotoLog, PPL: PDF Photo Logs). Fotolog was a big early photo-sharing site, which means uploading. In software, "log" means log files (Logwell is a logging platform).

**Does "shortlist" fight Never destructive? Yes.** The Oxford Learner's Dictionary defines a shortlist as "a small number of candidates… chosen from all the people who applied." In other words, keep a few and turn the rest away. That is the language of photo-culling apps (Canon Photo Culling, PhotoPicker). Round 15 warned about "pick" for the same reason. Keepsake does set many files aside, so a name that hints at cutting would make the "Nothing was deleted" screen harder to believe.

## Dropped (23)

- **The owner's three:** Photo log, Sortlist, Sortly (see above).
- **Log names:**
  - Piclog, Pixlog: Piclog and Picslog are photo-diary apps, and Pixlog also looks like Pixlr.
  - Daylog: the "Journal: Day Log" and "DayLog – Simple Journal" apps.
  - Datelog: heard as "Datalog", and "date" reads as dating (round 13).
  - Snaplog: a Snaplog product already exists, and the name points to Snapchat.
  - Monthlog: bullet-journal jargon.
  - Keeplog: truck and boat logbook apps, plus round 13's "keep" cleaner problem.
  - Logwell: a logging platform for developers, which is the technical meaning.
- **List names:**
  - Keeplist: the "Keeplist: Save in One Place" app.
  - Wholelist: crowded by to-do apps, heard as "hole list", and Whole Foods comes up.
  - Sortroll: Roll Sort puzzle games and a faceswap sort method.
  - Allsorted: the Sorted app, and Allsorts are sweets.
  - Sortbook: SortMyBooks bookkeeping software. "Book" reads as accounting or booking.
- **-ly names:**
  - Sortably: a SORTABLE mark, and it sounds like Sortly.
  - Sortedly: every search goes to Sortly.
  - Datedly, Dately: the Dately dating app, and "dated" also means old-fashioned.
  - Neatly: a Neatly productivity tool, plus the Neat brand (rounds 6 and 15).
  - Keeply: **Keeply – Hide photos & notes**, a photo-hiding app.

**Takeaway on -ly:** every "sort" + "-ly" name sits in Sortly's shadow. The ending is closed to us.

## The team's verdict

- **Photo log, Sortlist, Sortly: drop all three.** Sortly is the only one with real legal risk: the same classes, an active company and a photo-based organizing product. Sortlist is taken worldwide and its pun works against **Never destructive**. Photo log is generic and reads as paperwork or uploading.
- **The owner's ideas still help:**
  - The pun survives as **Longlist**, which says the opposite of shortlist.
  - The log idea is better used in copy than in the name. The receipt is already the app's record of what it did.
- **Best new name: Longlist,** as a reserve. It carries the owner's wordplay with an honest meaning, but it doesn't contain "sort", the word people search for (round 13).

## Owner suggestion, Nov 2 (evening): Sortnorg (sortn.org)

Team Lead's quick check, using web searches only. No product, app or mark called Sortnorg or "Sort n Org" turned up.

- **Neighbours:** "SORTN" is a Google Sheets function, and there are small "Sort N Search" and "Sort'n Fill" apps. Sortly (round 16) comes up in "sort" searches.
- **Hearing it:** heard aloud, it could be typed "Sort and Org", "Sortandorg" or "Sort-N-Org". The test must count all of these.
- **What "org" means:** in the Philippines, "org" usually means a school organization ("join an org"), not "organize". So "Sort n Org" may read as "sort and organization".
- **Reading it:** "Sortnorg" on its own can read as "sort-norg". The capital letters (SortnOrg) or the domain do the explaining.
- **The domain:** **sortn.org** spells the name across the dot, and .org fits an open-source project. The team couldn't check whether it's registered, because the lookup service is blocked here. People may type sortn.com or sortnorg.com, so those would need checking too.
- **Fit:** it says what the app does ("sort and organize"), with no cleaning, recovery or upload meaning. But it's closer to a description than a distinctive brand, so it may be harder to trademark.
