# Naming round 12: "snap" and other words for "photo" (Oct 28, 2026)

The owner asked for "brand names that involve 'snap' as in pictures, or other relevant replacements for 'photo'." The Product owner rated each replacement word, made up 41 names that pair those words with what the app does (sort, date, keep, home, set aside), and checked each one with web searches (apps, software brands, US marks in classes 9/42, photo, backup and recovery products). The list leaves out every name used or dropped in rounds 1–11, including Snapbubble, Snapcomb, Pixcomb, Photoreel, Filmstrip, Keepreel, Shoebox and Keepers. These are web searches, not legal clearance. The attorney search still comes before the beta (phase 4).

## Finding first: "Snap-" names are high risk

- **Snap Inc. opposes SNAP- names in our own goods.** It opposed SNAPNSTOR and SNAP-N-STOR, which were filed for *mobile phone photo software*, saying they were similar to SNAP and SNAPCHAT and would dilute SNAPCHAT. It also opposed SNAPSPACES (withdrawn in 2026 with the applicant's consent) and fought Panasonic over a camera-software logo. A SNAP- name for photo software is exactly what Snap's lawyers look for.
- **The best SNAP- compounds are already taken.** **SnapSort is an existing photo organizer** ("SnapSort: Foto-Organizer"). Snapseed (Google) and Snapfish (a cloud print service) own "snap + photo" in people's minds. For the family IT person, "snap" also means Canonical's Linux Snap packages.
- **"Snapshot" fights our promise.** To IT people a snapshot is a backup or restore point, so it suggests recovery.

**Result:** only one snap name got through the searches (Snapsake), and it is rated high risk.

## The team's top 2

1. **Stillwell.** It is the only name this round that makes the error line calmer ("Your files are fine" sits next to "still well"). It promises no recovery or cleaning, and its search is clean. Its weakness is that "still" isn't an everyday word for a photo in the Philippines, so it needs the descriptor: "Stillwell: photo sorter for recovered files".
2. **Printdrawer.** It is the warmest "home" picture in this round, but it must pass "Does it print your photos?"

Snapsake isn't recommended. The owner's "snap" direction runs into the one company most likely to object.

## How they compare with the current shortlist

- **Neither beats Photocomb or Ringwood.** Both of those say what the app does (combs through the pile; years in order). Stillwell says how it feels.
- **Stillwell vs Keeplight:** they are peers. Both are calm and need a descriptor, and Keeplight carries "keep" (**Never destructive**). Only add Stillwell to D21 if Keeplight fails the test.
- **Photofern** stays ahead of Printdrawer, with a cleaner search and no printing reading.
- **Keepbubble stays the reserve.** No change to the D21 test set is recommended.

## What to test (if added to D21, T44 rules)

- **"What do you think this app does?"** A name fails if 2 or more of 5 testers say it recovers, repairs, deletes, cleans or **prints** photos. For any snap name, also ask "Is this a Snapchat app?"
- **Hear it, type it.** Pass: 6 of 8 type it exactly, with at least 3 of 4 Filipino testers. This decides whether Stillwell or Stilwell gets typed.
- **Keepsake stays the control.**

The name is the owner's call (D21), and it must be settled before the beta (phase 4).
