# Naming round 8: around Porchlight, and Honeycomb hybrids (Oct 27, 2026)

The owner likes Porchlight but finds it a little vague, and asked for (A) names with the same feeling that say more about what the app does, and (B) Honeycomb-based names mixed with a light tech word. The Product owner made up 38 names and screened each one with web searches (apps, software brands, US marks in classes 9/42, photo, backup and recovery products). The list leaves out every name used or dropped in rounds 1–7, and anything close to PHOTO home. These are web searches, not legal clearance. The attorney search (US and Philippines) still comes before the beta (phase 4).

## Two findings first

1. **Porchlight is riskier than round 7 said.** Round 7 reported "no software product found". This round found:
   - **PORCH LIGHT, a registered US mark in class 42 software** (Reg. 6003049, RULISTING Inc., disaster-relief housing software).
   - Porchlight, a Mac app that changes the lock-screen message.
   - Porchlight Connect, a family check-in app.
   - PorchLight Design Studio, a real-estate app.

   Its risk goes from low–medium to **medium–high**.
2. **Honeycomb can't be used.**
   - It belongs to Honeycomb.io, the well-known observability company (Hound Technology, Inc.), and it was the name of Android 3.0.
   - It is also the name of a **private family app for photo and video stories**. Its HONEYCOMB application (Keep Life Stories, Inc.) covers classes 9 and 42. That is our field and our users.
   - "-comb" names that don't contain "Honey" stay open.

## Does a descriptor fix Porchlight?

Partly. "Porchlight: photo sorter for recovered files" fixes the vagueness in titles, app listings and search results. App sentences still just say "Porchlight", and the descriptor does nothing for the trademark (round 6). "Porchlight Photos" as the full name has the same conflicts, and it repeats itself in copy ("Porchlight Photos found 2,214 photos"). **Use a descriptor with whatever name wins, but it won't rescue Porchlight's trademark problem.**

## Dropped (29)

**A: light and homecoming (13)**
- **Taken or crowded:** Homelight (HomeLight real-estate marks), Lightkeeper (a Homey smart-home app), Nightlight (dozens of night-light apps), Lights On (photo-editing "Light on" features, and it sounds like a smart-home app).
- **Wrong meaning:**
  - Homeglow: Homeaglow is a house-cleaning service.
  - Welcome Mat: you wipe your feet on it.
  - Doorstep: means delivery, things sent and arriving.
  - Porchframe: "porch framing" is carpentry.
  - Window Lamp and Windowlight: sound like Microsoft Windows, and "window light" is a photography lighting term.
- **Weak:** Leave a Light On (four words, and a song title), Doorlight (not a word people use), Photo Porch (says "photo" twice in every sentence, and has the same Porch Group neighbour as Porch Album), Lamplit (its only mark is for lamps, class 11, but it is as vague as Porchlight and is heard as Lamplight, dropped in round 7).

**B: comb, hive and cell hybrids (16)**
- **Taken:** Honeycomb and Honeycomb Photos (finding 2), Combwise (a beekeeping-log app), PixHive and Photohive ("Hive – Image Organizer" and AlbumHive are existing photo apps), Hivefile (Apache Hive, hiveFS, and HiveDrive cloud storage).
- **Technical or wrong meaning:**
  - Combsort: a textbook sorting algorithm, so searches return only tutorials and npm packages, and it can't be protected.
  - Honeyfile: a security term for decoy files that catch intruders.
  - Honeystack: sounds like a honeypot, plus "tech stack".
  - Hexfile: the .hex file format and hex editors, and "hex" also means a curse.
  - Cellsort: lab cell sorters.
- **Other:** Honeycell (heard as Honeywell), Honeyframe (the wooden frame in a beehive; searches return beekeeping), Snapcomb (hair-clipper combs; Snapchat), Datecomb (reads like a dating app), Combfolder (clunky, and sounds like an instruction).

## The team's top 2

1. **Photocomb.** It is the only name in any round that says what the app does (goes through the pile for photos) while keeping a warm picture (the comb's cells). It holds no promise to recover, clean or upload, and the search is clean. Its weak spot is the silent b, so it must pass the hear-it-and-type-it test.
2. **Keeplight.** It keeps everything the owner liked about Porchlight (the light left on, home, warmth) without Porchlight's class 42 conflict. "Keep" says **Never destructive**, and it carries on naturally from Keepsake. It is no more specific than Porchlight, so it needs the descriptor: "Keeplight: photo sorter for recovered files".

Reserve: Lamplighter. Porchlight should now be tested only if the attorney clears PORCH LIGHT.

## What to test (add to D21)

- **"What do you think this app does?"** A name fails if 2 or more of 5 testers say it recovers, deletes, cleans, uploads or merges photos. For comb names also ask "Would it combine your photos?"; for Keeplight, "Is this a flashlight or lamp app?"; for Honeygrid, "Is this security software?"
- **Hear it, type it.** Pass: 6 of 8 type it exactly, with at least 3 of 4 Filipino testers (as in T44). This is the deciding test for Photocomb's silent b.
- **Keepsake stays the control.** Test no more than one comb name per session.

The name is the owner's call (D21). Renaming has to land before the beta (phase 4).
