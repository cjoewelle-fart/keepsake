# Naming round 17: bookmarks and marking down (Nov 8, 2026)

The owner wrote: "on the name, there are some inspiration i got from browsing on a book nook, i see there is a bookmark for each book and thought we can borrow that approach somehow since we kind of mark down galleries instead". She shared a list of visual bookmarking tools: Savee, Pinterest, Raindrop.io, Marqly, Dropmark, Niice and Curator. She asked for short coined names with a bookmark or "marking" idea. The UX writer made up 25 names in four groups. The list leaves out every name used or dropped in rounds 1–16 (Sortmark, Shelfmark, Placemark, Callmark, Datemark, Timemark, Bookend, Bookplate, Book Nook, Datestamp, Yearstamp, Photoleaf, Keepies and others).

**Important: no web searches were run this round.** The conflicts below are what the writer already knows, not a fresh screen. Every name still needs the usual web screen, the attorney's trademark search (US and Philippines, classes 9 and 42) and domain checks before it can be tested.

## Findings first

1. **"Bookmark" means "save it for later", usually in the cloud.** Savee, Pinterest, Raindrop.io, Dropmark and Marqly all keep your saved items on their servers. A bookmark-sounding name may make people think of an online service. That fights **Private by architecture**. It also fits badly with what the app does: sorting photos into dated folders on your own computer.
2. **In photo apps, "mark" means writing on the photo.** Watermark apps, date-stamp cameras (Timemark) and round 14's Datemark and Yearstamp problem all point the same way. A "-mark" name may make people think we write a date onto the photo. That fights "nothing changed". The test must ask about it.
3. **"Drop" in Dropmark and Raindrop means delete or upload.** You "drop" a file to upload it, and you "drop" a photo to get rid of it. Leave "drop" out.
4. **Spellings like Savee, Niice and Marqly are made for people who read them.** Those tools are for designers who see the name written down. Our users usually hear the name from a friend or a repair shop. Odd spellings fail hear-it, type-it by design.
5. **The bookmark idea already fits Keepsake.** A keepsake is often a pressed flower or a ticket kept between the pages of a book. A ribbon bookmark could be the logo for the current name.

## Tension check: which names say "your photos, back in order"?

| Says "your photos, in order" | Partly | Sounds only like a bookmark app |
|---|---|---|
| **Yeartab** (one tab per year: sorted and easy to find) | **Waymark** (in order along a path, but no photos or dates) | Keepmark, Pagemark, Dogear, Earmark, Leafmark, Nookmark, Ribbonmark |
| **Yearleaf** (one page per year) | **Daymark**, Notchwood, Tallymark (time marked, but not photos) | Markee, Ribbo, Tabbi, Marqo, Shelftab |

No name in this round says "photos" on its own. Every one of them needs the descriptor "Sort recovered photos by date" (round 13). The names with "year" in them come closest to what the app does. The pure bookmark names are about saving something to look at later. That isn't what the app does, and it suggests a cloud service. **Keep the owner's idea, but let it carry order, not saving.**

## Shortlist (the team's top 5)

| Name | Idea | In a sentence | Say / spell risk | Conflicts | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Yeartab** | Divider tabs, one per year: every photo filed behind its year | "Yeartab found 2,214 photos." "Yeartab can't save to that folder. Your files are fine." | Low–medium | None known. Browser-tab and bill meanings | Low–medium (not checked) |
| **Waymark** | Markers along a path: your years, marked along the way | "Waymark found 2,214 photos." | Low | Waymark (video ads), Waymarking.com | Medium |
| **Yearleaf** | A page for each year of your life | "Yearleaf found 2,214 photos." | Medium: "Yearleave" | None known | Low–medium (not checked) |
| **Keepmark** | A bookmark you keep. Closest to the owner's idea and to Keepsake | "Keepmark found 2,214 photos." | Medium: "Kipmark" | Google Keep, and the "keep" cleaner meaning | Medium |
| **Daymark** | A calm marker that shows the way | "Daymark found 2,214 photos." | Low | Daymark Solutions (IT) | Medium |

1. **Yeartab** is the best of the round. It keeps the owner's idea of putting a marker in a book, and it says what the app does: every year has its own tab. The words are plain, and no conflict is known.
2. **Waymark** is the calmest. The marker idea gives a sense of being guided. It says nothing about photos or dates, and there is a company with the same name.
3. **Yearleaf** says "one page per year" and fits with Yeartree. Test at most one tree or leaf name per session.
4. **Keepmark** is the owner's idea at its most direct. It keeps the "Keep-" sound from Keepsake. It sounds most like a bookmarking app, so it must pass "Does this app save links?"
5. **Daymark** is calm and easy to say, but vague. It needs the descriptor.

**Test rules (D21, T44), additions:**
- For any "-mark" or "-tab" name, ask "Does this app save links or web pages?" and "Would it write a date on your photos?" A name fails if 2 or more of 5 testers say yes to either.
- In hear-it, type-it, count "Yeartap", "Earmark" (for Yearmark), "Kipmark" and "Marquee" as fails.
- Test no more than one tree or leaf name per session (Yeartree, Yearleaf, Notchwood, Ringwood).

The name is the owner's call (D21), and it must be settled before the beta (phase 4).
