# Naming round 14: library words (Oct 28, 2026)

The owner asked us to "think more about terms related to library: organizing shelves in the library by categories, Dewey Decimal, etc." The Product owner matched library and archive words to features and feelings, made up 41 names, and checked each one with web searches (apps, software brands, US marks in classes 9/42, photo, backup and library software). The list leaves out every name used or dropped in rounds 1–13 (Almanac, Daybook, Quire, Steward, Bookend, Photo Librarian, Shelfwise, Sortshelf, Photoshelf, Picshelf, Restack and others). These are web searches, not legal clearance. The attorney search still comes before the beta (phase 4).

## Finding first: shelving is honest, the rest of the library is crowded

Reshelving is the closest library match to what the app does. Each item goes back to its one right place, and nothing is thrown away or changed (**Never destructive**). It doesn't promise to get anything back, so it avoids "restore". The returns cart (books that came back, waiting to be shelved) is a lovely picture of a PhotoRec folder. As a name, though, it means shop returns. Most other library words are already taken by library software (Ex Libris, Koha, Evergreen, Libib, OCLC) or mean something we must avoid.

## Map: library words, matched to features and feelings

| Word | Feature or feeling | Fit |
|---|---|---|
| Shelving, reshelving | Each photo put back in its date place | **Best.** Careful: "shelve" also means "put off" |
| Returns cart | The recovery folder: items back, waiting to be shelved | **Best as a picture.** As a name it means e-commerce returns |
| Call number, Dewey Decimal | Each item has one place; the number is the date ("2024-06-28 14.32") | Good idea. **DEWEY is OCLC's mark** (it sued the Library Hotel over it). "Call" means a phone call |
| Shelf-mark, spine label | The place written on each item | Good idea, but library jargon. Shelfmark is taken |
| Card catalog, index cards, finding aid | An index of everything found (results, receipt) | Medium. "Catalog" sounds like shopping, "index" sounds technical, "aid" sounds medical |
| Stacks, aisles, sections, volumes, spines | Rows by year and month; each year a volume | Medium. Google Photos' "Stacks" groups similar photos. "Volume" also means sound and disks |
| Bookplate, ex libris | "These are yours" | Good feeling; both taken |
| Accession | Adding to a collection (saving) | Weak: jargon |
| Circulation, date due, card pocket | Lending | **Fights Private by architecture:** lending means sending out. "Due" means a deadline |
| Date stamp | Dates | **Fights "nothing changed":** stamp apps print the date onto photos |
| Reading room, the librarian's "shh" | Calm, private | Feeling only. "Hush" means vault apps (round 2) |
| Archive, acid-free box | Kept for good | **Fights:** means backup or cold storage |
| Folio, codex, colophon, register, ledger | The book itself | Weak or taken. "Ledger" means accounting and crypto, even though our UI uses a ledger |
| Library | The dated library | Descriptor only. It's Apple's own word ("Photos library") |

## Dropped (36)

- **Taken in our field:**
  - Photostacks, Stacks: the PhotoStacks organizer app, and Google Photos' "Photo Stacks".
  - Photofolio, Folio: PhotoFolio portfolio apps and Folio photo apps.
  - Photo Catalog, Card Catalog: a Photo Catalog app. Descriptive.
  - Datestamp, Daystamp: a Datestamp photo date-stamp app and a Daystamp habit tracker.
  - Photocart: Photo Cart, software for selling photos.
  - Photoledger: PhotoLedger software.
  - Bookplate: a Japanese library-search app.
  - Photoplate: Plates, an album app for photographers. In the Philippines "plate" also means a dish or a car plate.
- **Taken elsewhere:**
  - Shelfmark: a self-hosted book downloader.
  - Ex Libris: Clarivate's library-software company.
  - Dewey: OCLC's mark.
  - Codex: OpenAI.
  - Ledger: the crypto wallet.
  - Cardpocket: a card-wallet app.
  - Placemark: an open-source map tool.
  - Carrel: an AI writing tool.
  - Callmark: CALLMARK Solutions, a call-centre firm.
  - Accession: collection-management software, and obscure.
  - Archivebox: ArchiveBox, a web-archiving tool.
  - Shelver, Photoshelver: Shelver, a home-organization app.
  - Libris: Libris software and Sweden's library catalogue.
  - Book Nook: reading apps, and cute.
  - Cardfile, Index Card: Windows Cardfile and index-card apps.
  - Colophon: software tools, and obscure.
- **Wrong meaning:**
  - Call Number: phone-dialer apps.
  - Returns Cart: Newgistics ReturnCart; means sending things back to shops.
  - Circulation, Date Due: lending and deadlines.
  - Shelf Life: expiry-date trackers.
  - Volumes: sound and disks.
  - Stackroom: heard as "stockroom" (Commusoft Stockroom).
  - Yearstamp: only office rubber stamps turned up, but it reads as "prints the year on your photos".
  - Finding Aid, Spinelabel, Spineline: jargon. SpineLine is also a medical journal.
- **Generic or weak:**
  - Reading Room, Register: generic.

## The team's top 2

1. **Reshelf.** It's the only library word that says what the app does (put each photo where it belongs) without promising recovery, and its search is clean. The returns cart gives it a story and a logo. It must pass "Would this app move or hide your photos?" and "Does it get deleted photos back?" (**Calm and plain**, **Never destructive**).
2. **Shelfwell.** It's the library cousin of Sortwell, gentle and with a clean search, but it carries no search term. It only matters if Sortwell fails.

## How they compare with the current test set

- **Reshelf vs Unjumble:** both are literal. Unjumble names the mess and Reshelf names the result. Unjumble has a word-game risk and Reshelf has a "restore" risk. Don't test them in one session. **Put Reshelf next in line, ahead of Photofern and Keeplight.** Keeplight now carries round 13's "keep" cleaner risk.
- **Reshelf vs Photocomb:** Photocomb says the search ("combs through"), Reshelf says the putting away. Reshelf has no silent b. It's the natural replacement if Photocomb fails hear-it-type-it.
- **Shelfwell vs Sortwell:** Sortwell stays. "Sort" is the word people search for, and the subtitle "Sort recovered photos by date" backs it up. Shelfwell is a backup only; never test both.
- **Ringwood:** stays ahead of Yearshelf and Datemark on the "years in order" idea.
- **Library words in copy:** the returns-cart and shelf pictures can be used for the logo and empty screens under any name (visual direction, phase 3). Keep "library" in the descriptor.

## What to test (if added to D21, T44 rules)

- **"What do you think this app does?"** A name fails if 2 or more of 5 testers say it recovers, deletes, cleans, uploads or **lends** photos. For Reshelf, also ask "Would it move or hide your photos?" For any shelf name, ask "Is this a bookshop, furniture or library app?"
- **Hear it, type it.** Pass: 6 of 8 type it exactly, with at least 3 of 4 Filipino testers.
- **Keepsake stays the control.**

The name is the owner's call (D21), and it must be settled before the beta (phase 4).
